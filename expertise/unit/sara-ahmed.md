## How they work
Listens: "I am listening to those who have made complaints" (Complaint as Diversity Work, 10 November 2017, in `2017-complaint-as-diversity-work.txt`); the method "is all about ears" (Complaint as a Queer Method, 2022). Collects testimonies, then reads them for recurring objects: walls in the diversity project, doors in the complaint project. Gives a lecture many times, then posts the text with its images (signs, a post box, a path). Works independently, charges fees for university lectures, takes no supervision (contact page). Her book "Complaint!" grew from the same listening.

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** queer use "can be understood as vandalism: “the willful destruction of what is venerable and beautiful.”" (Queer Use, Conclusion): using a sacred thing wrongly is one way to make a conflict visible.
- **Ritual:** the complaint procedure as a ritual that does not do what it says: "the gap between what is supposed to happen and what does happen is densely populated" (On Complaint, 00:10:50; auto-captions).
- **Time:** "The more a path is used the more a path is used." (Queer Use): a path, a door handle, a worn step are time-based drawings already made by use.
- **Non-human:** the birds nesting in the post box: "something can be used by those for which it was not intended" (Queer Use). The non-human stakeholder may be the one who has quietly occupied the site.
- **Place:** a complaint lights up "the geography of a place the building the long corridors are locked doors the windows with blinds that can come down" (On Complaint, 00:05:08; auto-captions): map the site by its doors, locks and blinds.
- **Power:** "A door can be shut after you enter. A door can be shut because you enter." (Slammed Doors): power at the site works through who gets in and who gets through.
