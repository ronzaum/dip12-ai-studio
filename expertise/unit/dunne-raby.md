## Positions
- "Critical Design uses speculative design proposals to challenge narrow assumptions, preconceptions and givens about the role products play in everyday life. It is more of an attitude than anything else, a position rather than a method." ("Critical Design FAQ", 2007) — in `2007-critical-design-faq.txt`
- "The viewer should experience a dilemma, is it serious or not? Real or not?" ("Critical Design FAQ", 2007) — in `2007-critical-design-faq.txt`
- "Too weird and it will be dismissed as art, too normal and it will be effortlessly assimilated." ("Critical Design FAQ", 2007) — in `2007-critical-design-faq.txt`
- "What if design education's focus on 'making stuff real' perpetuates everything that is wrong with current reality, ensuring that all possible futures are merely extrapolations of a dysfunctional present?" (Anthony Dunne, "A Larger Reality", 2018) — in `2018-a-larger-reality-anthony-dunne.txt`
- "there's another space of speculation that can be used not to imagine better products but to imagine better human relations or to critique a political situation" (AA School lecture, published 2015, auto-captions, 01:07:43–01:07:51) — in `2015-aa-school-lecture-captions.txt`

## How they work
- Objects as props for a question. Working prototypes or mock-ups in the look of ordinary consumer goods: "nothing we've ever designed has been mass-produced but we like to sort of use the language of mass production or everyday consumerism" (AA lecture auto-captions, 00:13:16–00:13:24). They stop once the idea is plausible; production is not the point: "almost as if you don't put something into production it's not real" (AA lecture auto-captions, 01:08:08–01:08:11).
- Tone: satire, deadpan, black humour; not parody. The aim is a dilemma for the viewer, not a message.
- Collaborators: scientists, an economist, and graduate students (RCA, Parsons); the recent turn is "speculative economics or speculative ethics" given form through objects, systems and services (AA lecture auto-captions, 01:11:45).
- Time: a studio since 1994; ideas run across decades (critical design named in *Hertzian Tales*, 1999; the FAQ, 2007; *Speculative Everything*, 2013; *Not Here, Not Now*, 2025).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** the FAQ draws the line at the everyday: critical design "needs to be closer to the everyday, that's where its power to disturb comes from". A profanation that is too strange reads as art and is dismissed.
- **Ritual:** their objects test a belief: what would you live with? Build a prop for your ritual and set it in the familiar language of products, then watch who accepts it.
- **Time:** "A Larger Reality" argues that designing for the present reproduces it; futures should not be "merely extrapolations of a dysfunctional present". A ritual drawn through time can hold a counterfactual.
- **Power:** speculation "to critique a political situation" rather than make better products. The FAQ names the opposite: "affirmative design: design that reinforces the status quo".
