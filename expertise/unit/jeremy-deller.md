## How they work
- Research first, then people. For Orgreave: a year in archives and interviews, trial transcripts, police statements and surveillance photos from a lawyer, miners' own picket-line photographs, then meeting those involved ("I had done my homework, basically"). Over 800 participants, many former miners and a few former police, plus battle re-enactment societies; the script written by re-enactment expert Howard Giles and changed a week before the day because the evidence showed the police had provoked the riot.
- Three outputs from one event: "The performance was the artwork and the film was a piece of documentation. The book was an attempt to gather a lot of the research together and make a decent history book out of an art project." (Photoworks, 2011)
- Delegates the making: "I suppose I work with groups of people and communities I do it because you can't get things done if you don't work with people" (Louisiana Channel, 2023, 00:03:23–00:03:30); compares his role to a film director relying on other people's skills.
- A decoy helps. Artangel's executive producer: "With Deller's idea, it was clear that the decoy of a film would be necessary", for finance and "a certain degree of credibility" with the owners of locations (Michael Morris, "Making The Battle of Orgreave", Artangel, June 2002).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** Sacrilege, a bouncy Stonehenge, is named to get the charge of profanation in first: "The title is a way is to ward off any criticism—some will think that it is just that, a sacrilege, so why not call it that?" (Artforum, 2012). He treats art as the thing that now "can offer personal salvation" where religion once did.
- **Ritual:** Orgreave is a re-enactment of a conflict: a one-day ritual with 800 people, a script, costumes and a cavalry charge, done on or near the original site. Useful model for the ritual section and the film: event, documentation, book.
- **Time:** the gap matters. Orgreave was restaged seventeen years on; he wanted re-enactors to work beside "veterans of a recent confrontation" so that "living history" was literal.
- **Place:** Stonehenge for him is "the most contemporary building in Britain because there's always speculation there's always something happening there" (Louisiana Channel, 2023, 00:10:49–00:10:54): a ruin that stays live because it is argued over.
- **Power:** the strike as "a civil war between the North and the South of the country"; the state, the police and the press are the other side in the re-enactment. The council "refused to meet us on any level".
