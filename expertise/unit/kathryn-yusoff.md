## How they work
Reads across disciplines and says so: "I engage historical, geophilosophical and black feminist methods" (QMUL profile). The Preface names who she thinks with: Hartman, Césaire, Glissant, Wynter, Spillers, McKittrick, Jemisin, Dionne Brand ("kept me company at 2:00 A.M."). Works from archives and geology's own vocabulary (strata, Golden Spikes, the inhuman) and turns them back on the discipline. Teaches a New York field trip and "Colonial Lives & Afterlives" (QMUL profile). Talks to architects directly (Yale, Harvard GSD).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** in the 2019 essay the earth that enslaved people held in "great veneration" stands against land and labour as property; the sacred ground is the one the owner cannot title.
- **Ritual:** the provision plot: "the plot accorded to slaves to grow their own food" as a daily practice that undoes the plantation (2019 essay, line 33).
- **Time:** the Anthropocene's start date is a choice with a body count: 1610 is tied to "the death of 50 million indigenous people" (2019 essay). A timeline that picks a Golden Spike picks whose history counts.
- **Non-human:** her word is "the inhuman": both matter and the people made into matter. A non-human stakeholder is never neutral here.
- **Place:** "where does architecture begin and where does it end" (Yale, 00:04:48) if every wall is a severed mine; the site drawing should show where the material came from.
- **Power:** "Geology is a mode of accumulation" for one side and "dispossession" for the other; "at its onset geology is always about race and earth" (Yale, 00:21:03).
