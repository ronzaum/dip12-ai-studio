## How they work
Learned rhythm by ear from rappers and MCs; writes poems "from the same place, rhythmically, as I would write lyrics", and is now looking for a different musicality on the page. Poems arrive singly, group themselves around themes, then a title appears and the collection shows what it wants to be. *Brand New Ancients* was a 75-minute poem written "over a period of many months, in weeklong bursts of intense creativity", scored for drums and electronics, violin, cello and tuba, then learned by heart and toured; the text taught its own author things night by night. The second novel took about three years, at a friend's house and in two residencies in Italy and Spain. Theatre with director Ian Rickson (*Paradise*, National Theatre, 2021). The test of the work is the room: writer, text and reader as three stations of one circuit.

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** On stage during *Europe Is Lost*: "i beat my chest when i'm on stage and i sway", saying "we have sinned", "we are lost" (2021 conversation, auto-captions, 00:44:29): a secular show carrying a liturgical gesture.
- **Ritual:** Touring the same text every night and discovering "Patterns to the language that I hadn’t been aware of when I was writing" (2020 essay): repetition as the method, which fits the unit's one-ritual film.
- **Time:** "Between the day that everyone in attendance had brought into the room with them, and the prospect of the night to come" (2020 essay): the performance as the hinge in a day; a time-based drawing of a gig.
- **Place:** Catford and the people known "my whole life here" (2022 interview); "the club was the place" (2021 conversation, auto-captions, 00:08:14).
- **Power:** "I don’t understand how my body, our bodies, became a territory for war" (2022 interview); who gets to speak about trans lives in the press.
