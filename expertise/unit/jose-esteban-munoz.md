## How they work
Reads a draft chapter aloud and shows clips: "I'm just going to read it. I'll talk through some of it. I have clips that I'll show you" (00:04:31). Takes one concept from elsewhere (Raymond Williams's "structure of feeling") and tests it on performers: a 1950s Cuban singer, a present-day Cuban/Puerto Rican performance artist, the bolero as a song form. Treats nightlife, song and off-off-Broadway shows as the evidence. Also programmed film: a show called "Retention" for the New York Experimental Film Festival (00:12:18). Edited the journals Social Text and Women and Performance (Fales finding aid).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** what the mainstream calls "too gay", "too much", an "excess of feeling and emotion of particularity" (00:11:39–00:11:42), is what gets cleaned out; at your site, ask which feeling is treated as excess and kept out of the sacred space.
- **Ritual:** nightlife and performance are where "a social fabric is weaved" (00:29:56): a nightly show, a song sung in a bar, can be the ritual you film.
- **Time:** lullabies and boleros as "longing for another time and place" (00:30:59): a ritual can be a way of mourning a lost past in the present.
- **Place:** "displaced not only from an island or a place, but also from a dominant sexuality" (00:32:00): displacement is from a place and from a norm at once.
- **Power:** "Expanded representation for any minority does not equal economic justice or anything like civil rights." (00:05:56): visibility is not the same as power at the site.
