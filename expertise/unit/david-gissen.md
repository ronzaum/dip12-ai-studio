## How they work
History first, then a made thing. The Vendôme project reconstructs a mound of hay, sand and manure the Commune built in 1871 to cushion the fall of a column; a research assistant, Samuel Garcia, worked the National Archives in Paris (CCA talk, 00:17:00). For the Acropolis he teamed up with "a Greek archaeologist" and a classical art historian to make small artifacts: a lost seat at the top of the ramp, "reconstructed it three different ways", shown at the Venice Biennale 2021 (Off-Centre, 00:25:24–00:26:21). In studio: "I have a Prohibition against ramps in any project" (00:12:16). Design collaborators Irene Cheng and Brett Snyder ("disabled communalism", e-flux 2024). Also curates (Venice Biennale, CCA, National Building Museum; Yale page).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** *Subnature* is a table of the unclean: "pt. 1. Dankness -- Smoke -- Gas -- Exhaust -- pt. 2. Dust -- Puddles -- Mud -- Debris -- pt. 3. Weeds -- Insects -- Pigeons -- Crowds." (CCA record). The profane matter a site is cleaned of.
- **Ritual:** the Commune's demolition of the Vendôme column, with a mound built to catch it, is a collective act he rebuilds as an earthwork; a model for filming one event and drawing its apparatus.
- **Time:** reconstruction is his method: which natures "become reconstructed and historicized in our cities today and why that happens". The seat done three ways because "nobody knows what it looks like".
- **Place:** build "out and across properties" (e-flux 2024): Berkeley easements, Ridgewood one-storey lots; access as a shared boundary, not a ramp.
- **Power:** "the plantation and the reservation" as landscapes designed to incapacitate (e-flux 2024); property is the conflict, access is only one use of it.
