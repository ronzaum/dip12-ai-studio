## How they work
Short newspaper essays written fast after an event (Le Monde, seven weeks after 11 September; NLR, weeks after the November 2005 fires), then expanded into small books. Starts from the object, not the idea: a corner divan, a wall, a burning car, two towers. Reads an event as a symbolic exchange (gift, challenge, sacrifice, debt) rather than a cause and effect. Lectures in English at EGS, apologising for it first. Translated by others (Rachel Bloul, James Benedict, Sheila Faria Glaser, Chris Turner). Says of analysis: "when they speed up, one must slow down".

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** the burning cars become "a sort of eternal flame, like that under the Arc de Triomphe"; the attack is "symbolic and sacrificial death". He finds the sacred inside the profane act, not opposite it.
- **Ritual:** "ninety cars on average are torched every night in this gentle France of ours", a nightly norm. A ritual can be a repeated act of destruction with its own count.
- **Time:** "when they speed up, one must slow down". Also his reading of the "events strike" of the 1990s: a timeline can have a gap in it.
- **Place:** rooms "have been liberalized" and walls no longer make "spaces of refuge" (System of Objects extract); the towers, "in their very double-ness", embody an order. Buildings carry the symbolic stake.
- **Power:** "And power is complicit with its own destruction." "The spectacle of terrorism imposes the terrorism of the spectacle." Ask what your conflict's images do to the event.
