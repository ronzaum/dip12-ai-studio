## How they work
The book was written together over about five years ("a five-year project", 00:16:37); the aim of co-writing is a "third thing between you" (00:16:53). Fictioning is run as a durational practice, "without a set beginning or ending" (p. 6). Plastique Fantastique performances use props, objects and electronic devices in collective performance (00:21:10–00:21:23); a performance is restaged at other sites and galleries; a book is treated as "itself a kind of device" that works when it finds the right place or reader (00:22:07). O'Sullivan's recent writing starts from landscape, walking and found objects (the lecture's first half).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** fictioning is a speech act that changes relations without touching matter: the judge's "guilty", the "I do" of a marriage (p. 5). A ritual can be read as such an enunciation.
- **Ritual:** an artist's pilgrimage to a well, a ritual performed there, then restaged in a Peckham gallery (00:40:00): one ritual, two sites, "activation of that scene across space and across time".
- **Time:** time as a material that accrues across works (p. 84); a practice "without a set beginning or ending" (p. 6). Time-based drawings can show the accretion, not only the event.
- **Non-human:** the book's stated theme includes "future human and non-human modes of existence" (p. 1).
- **Place:** a practice "activates the landscape and activates different temporalities caught in the landscape" (00:40:36): a site as layered time, not a backdrop.
- **Power:** fictioning "can take on a critical power when it is set against, or foregrounded within, a given reality" (p. 2); the counter-fiction is pitched inside the conflict, not outside it.
