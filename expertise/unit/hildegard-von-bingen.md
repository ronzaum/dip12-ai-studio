## How they work
Sees, then dictates. The vision comes awake, "with the eyes of the spirit"; the monk Volmar writes it down (Lerman; Singer, n. 32). Three books of visions over thirty years: *Scivias* 1141–50, *Liber Vitae Meritorum* 1158–62, *Liber Divinorum Operum* 1163–70 (Singer, section V). Each vision is first described, then explained; the later book re-reads the disk seen "twenty-eight years before". Pictures are part of the method: the Wiesbaden codex miniatures, probably made under her supervision, show the concentric lights she describes (Singer, section X). She asks permission before she speaks: the letter to Bernard asks "whether I should speak these things openly or keep my silence" (Epistolae). Materials: the body itself, illness of thirty days ending with a voice saying "Arise!" (Vita, in Singer, section X); plants, stones and animals in the *Physica*; a sung play, *Ordo Virtutum*, performed in her own convent (Lerman).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** The vision has to be authorised: she writes to Bernard asking whether to speak or stay silent; he passes it to Pope Eugenius, who tells her to finish the book (Epistolae; Lerman). The conflict is who may see and who may say.
- **Ritual:** *Ordo Virtutum*, her music and moral play, with some evidence it was performed in her own convent (Lerman; score on IMSLP). A ritual with a cast, a score and a building.
- **Time:** The same disk seen twice, twenty-eight years apart, and the second book written against the first (Singer, section VII). A time-based drawing can be one image redrawn across a life.
- **Non-human:** Winds, stars and "vapours" descend on men, beasts and fruit; the aether "resisted these ashes and vapours" (Singer, section VII). The non-human stakeholder is the weather.
- **Place:** Around 1150 she moved her convent from Disibodenberg, where the nuns lived alongside the monks, to Bingen on the Rhine (Lerman). The site is the move itself.
- **Power:** A woman "untaught and untrained" asking a famous abbot whether she may speak (Epistolae). Power in the conflict runs through the right to publish.
