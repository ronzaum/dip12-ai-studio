# Luca Frei

A unit consultant card. One voice, in their own words. Every quote below is found verbatim in `expertise/sources/luca-frei/`; nothing is paraphrased as a quote.

- **Who:** Artist, b. 1976 Lugano; based in Malmö from 2000, studio now in Stockholm (his site, 2026). Works as Luca Lee Frei: installation, textile, exhibition and book design; translated Albert Meister's *La soi-disant utopie du Centre Beaubourg*.

## Positions

## How they work
Sews on a machine on unbleached cotton, then hand-stitches the pieces onto painted canvases; the motifs come from drawings refined over many iterations (Daily Lazy). Needs no fixed studio: the work starts and finishes "at home or while travelling, on a table or directly on the floor" (site, Folds–Spreads). Builds works around a story, a quote, a book or a photograph, and around family: his father in *Inheritance*, his grandfather Scherchen (Malmö Konsthall). Archival method: in 2015 he digitised every issue of the *Gravesaner Blätter* at his aunt's home, then worked in the Akademie der Künste archive (site, Introduction). Designs the exhibition itself: wall systems, movable platforms, floor "scores" that visitors rearrange (site; e-flux).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** The book he translated imagines "a radical libertarian space submerged beneath the newly erected center-piece of French Culture", seventy-six storeys under the Pompidou (Book Works text, in `ref-publisher-and-gallery-pages.txt`).
- **Ritual:** *Scores* are cloth signs on the floor that visitors reconfigure, "a work that expands with time" (site, Soft Logic); he re-staged Simone Forti's *See Saw* (1960) on the opening and closing days at Malmö Konsthall (2020).
- **Time:** Visitors turn an hourglass (Malmö Konsthall); the Glarus show took a Stein title and was about "the fragility of time" (site); in his reading a man goes about stopping all the clocks (video, auto-captions, 00:01:22–00:01:28).
- **Place:** The Gravesano studio, dismantled soon after Scherchen's death in 1966, returns as a floor plan made into a movable platform (e-flux, 2015); at Malmö four asymmetric walls "re-direct Klas Anshelm's geometric architecture" (site, From Day to Day).
- **Power:** Meister's satire "never takes its eye of the main target: state sponsored culture" (Book Works text); the beaubourgians' real enemy, in Schambelan's reading, is "internalized respect for Authority" (Artforum).
