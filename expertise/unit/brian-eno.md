## How they work
Sets up a process and lets it run: a few elements that "interact and cluster and permutate" (2003 talk); music built so no instrument leads, the pyramid "squashed down" (2003 talk, opening). Bans options when producing: "one of the things I spend quite a lot of time doing is banning options" (RBMA 2013). Collaborators: Danny Hillis and Stewart Brand on the 10,000-year clock, for which he wrote the chimes from English change ringing (2003 talk); Peter Schmidt on the cards; Bette Adriaanse on the 2025 book. Makes music "as an antidote to the place I was living in" (2003 talk). Sings backing vocals and in choirs; was in a Brooklyn gospel choir for five years as "the only white atheist member" (RBMA 2013). Time horizon: the clock, 10,000 years; an artwork as a seed that needs "a viewer's (or a whole culture's) active mind in which to develop" (essay 2000).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** An atheist who says the music that moves him most is religious, and names what he hears: people "there to receive something" (RBMA 2013). Sex, drugs, art and religion are all "surrender situations" (RBMA 2013). A ritual space can be designed for surrender, not control.
- **Ritual:** The choir: a weekly act done badly on purpose, voices that "wobble about all over the place and dive through the other singers" (RBMA 2013). Church bells and change ringing as the sound of a long now (2003 talk).
- **Time:** New College, Oxford: oaks planted when the hall was built so that the beams could be replaced five hundred years later (2003 talk, Q&A). The time-based drawing can run for centuries. "The longer your sense of Now, the more past and future it includes" (essay 2000).
- **Place:** The 1978 New York loft whose hostess said the neighbourhood was "outside": the "Small Here" (essay 2000). Draw the Here your site actually includes.
- **Power:** Industries plan to the shareholders' meeting, politicians to the poll, media to the reaction: a "studied disregard of the future" (essay 2000). Who in your conflict holds the longest now?
