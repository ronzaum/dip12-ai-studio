## Positions

## How they work
Builds in the Unity game engine, in C#, and wants the technology well supported and "almost invisible" so the viewer watches a culture, not a tech demo (Louisiana, 00:13:19–00:14:08). Gives each character several competing models of mind: a threat model from first-person shooters, a needs model from The Sims, a narrative model after Julian Jaynes; only one gets the "spotlight" at a time (Louisiana, 00:03:08–00:04:35). Every rock, face and plant is made unique, procedural generation fused with hand animation, after Miyazaki (Louisiana, 00:11:07–00:12:19). Sets a premise and goals, then "losing control and not being overly deterministic" (Louisiana, 00:06:55). The work runs without beginning or end; a viewer can look for five seconds or five hours (Louisiana, 00:16:13).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** The *Emissaries* premise comes from Jaynes: ancient people heard voices they took for gods; the emissary, a shaman's daughter, is hit on the head and "stops hearing the voices that the father hears" and starts to think alone (Louisiana, 00:01:15, 00:05:47).
- **Ritual:** One agent holds a script, "a set of narrative goals", inside an ecosystem that reacts; the drama is the plan against the environment (Serpentine, 00:01:56–00:02:36). A ritual filmed over time is the same contest.
- **Time:** Three simulations in one landscape "many thousands of years apart", built "so that you never had a beginning an end" (Louisiana, 00:00:11). A ritual that loops needs the same design.
- **Non-human:** Nature is the container he needs because "we don't expect of nature total determinism"; a city or an architecture would be "such deterministic things" (Louisiana, 00:12:24–00:13:09). The second emissary is a dog.
- **Place:** The site persists while the group changes: a volcano, then a crater turned garden, then an AI that is a substance "like water or gas" (Louisiana, 00:05:18–00:08:51).
- **Power:** "A World has laws. A World has values." and "gives its members permission to act differently than outside of it" (Raga 2). The political dimension he claims is only "that maybe embracing certain kinds of change is actually more healthy than not" (Louisiana, 00:15:56).
