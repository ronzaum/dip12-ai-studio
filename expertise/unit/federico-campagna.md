## How they work
Lectures without slides of his own buildings: he reads other people's worlds. Method: take a cultural product (a building, a poem, a game) and ask what it implies about what exists, what is possible, how time moves (AA talk, 00:28:22 to 00:30:38). He sets this as an exercise for students: "ask yourself what are the implicit things there are included" in what you are designing (AA talk, 00:30:59). Examples come from "ancient or archaic or traditional architects" (AA talk, 00:38:06): Egyptian funerary temples, Hindu theories of sacrifice, Rykwert's *The Idea of a Town*. Works in books, a podcast and publishing (Verso; co-founded the Italian publisher Timeo, per the SAS profile). Collaborators named in the material: Andrea Bellini (Flash Art), Franco "Bifo" Berardi (afterword to *Prophetic Culture*, per the BL record).

## Hooks to the brief
- **Sacred / profane:** For him the sacred is not honoured but re-enacted: "the sacrifice is a reproduction of the activity through which the gods keep the world spinning" (AA talk, 00:39:27). Ask which activity your ritual reproduces, and what it keeps spinning.
- **Ritual:** A ritual as "a reproduction of a sacred event" (AA talk, 00:39:23): film it as a re-making of something, not as a tribute to it.
- **Time:** Every building carries a "time frame of things" (AA talk, 00:30:38): Egyptian tombs and Costa del Sol villas imply different temporalities. Your time-based drawings can state the building's implied time.
- **Non-human:** The Egyptian "false door" is "a door that allows the spirits to pass in and out of the building" (AA talk, 00:44:25); he doubts today's buildings provide "ways of passage for immaterial" entities (00:44:46). Which entities does your detail admit?
- **Power:** "politics is the art of the possible" and the range of the possible is set by a world's metaphysics (AA talk, 00:34:05). The power in your conflict may sit in what is declared impossible.
