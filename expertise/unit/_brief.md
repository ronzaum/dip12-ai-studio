# The brief: Book of Rituals

The unit brief as a voice. When a question touches what the unit asks for, `/consult` brings this in alongside the other voices. Every quote below is from `project/brief-dip12.md`.

- **Who:** Diploma Unit 12, Architectural Association
- **Work:** *Project Log: Book of Rituals*, Term 1, 2026–2027. Deadline Week 1, Term 2; edited into a book in weeks 8 and 9 of Term 1
- **Full text:** `project/brief-dip12.md`
- **Status:** suggested by unit

## What it asks for
- **A book as evidence.** "a project log in which you will compile all the evidence of the context, the conflict, the ritual, and the design process of your project, as well as your references."
- **Context, with three required drawings.** "a stakeholder diagram", "scaled drawing(s) of site", "a timeline". The group can be human or not: "the social group or non-human entity you are addressing".
- **A conflict set against the sacred and the profane.** It "should reflect the relationship between the sacred and the profane with the conflict", evidenced through "the power dynamics and frictions that generate the conflict".
- **A research question in a set form.** "In the context of… [include the where, the who], how can I/this project….[include the what: practice technique/methodology] to bring about …[describe the change you want to bring about]."
- **One ritual that makes the conflict visible.** It "shows the ways in which sacred spaces and regimes are reinforced or profaned." "Must include: time based drawings", and the section collects the materials for "the making of a film presenting the chosen ritual."
- **Expertise in three parts.** Design ("Must include: resolution of a detail"); theory and knowledge, from "the institutional and academic to any forms of collective intelligence, lived experience and traditional or indigenous knowledge"; and "references of practices that influence your own practice."
- **A working tool, not a final object.** "It is a working tool and should be brought to tutorials regularly to facilitate discussion and contextualization of the project."

## Questions it puts to any project
- Who or what is the stakeholder, and is it human?
- Where are the sacred and the profane in this conflict, and who holds power?
- Does the research question follow the form: context, method, change?
- Which one ritual makes the conflict visible, and how does it unfold in time?
- Which detail will the design resolve?
- Is there evidence for each claim, in a form that can be printed and brought to a tutorial?
