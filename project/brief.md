# Project brief

Write this yourself. Short and true beats long and polished. Claude reads it before every answer about your project.

It follows the unit brief, the Book of Rituals (`project/brief-dip12.md`): Context, Conflict, Ritual, Expertise. One or two lines per heading is enough to start. The evidence itself lives in the folders named under each one.

## Title

## Context
Who, what, where, when. Evidence in `project/site/` and `project/research/`.

- **Who:** the social group or non-human entity you are addressing. Required: **stakeholder diagram**.
- **What:** the architectures, objects and materials that define the site.
- **Where:** the site and where it is. Required: **scaled drawing(s) of the site**.
- **When:** key history, and what may come next. Required: **timeline**.

## Conflict
The power and friction at your site, and how it relates to the sacred and the profane.

- **Title:** evocative and catchy
- **Subtitle:** descriptive and concise
- **Research question:** In the context of … [where, who], how can I / this project … [what: practice, technique, methodology] to bring about … [the change you want].
- **Statement:** 300 words. The research question expanded: the conflict, the urgency, the 5W, the project strategy.

## Ritual
One ritual that makes the conflict visible. Evidence in `project/ritual/`.

- **Who:** who enacts it
- **What:** the technologies that enable it
- **Where:** the exact location(s)
- **When:** how it unfolds over time. Required: **time-based drawings**.
- **Why:** the motivations behind it, from first-hand witnesses
- **Film:** what the film of the ritual will show

## Expertise
One line each for now. Detail in `expertise/`.

- **Design:** materials, tools, fabrication, craft; the detail you will resolve
- **Knowledge:** the texts, institutions and experts you are learning from
- **Practice:** the practices that shape how you work
